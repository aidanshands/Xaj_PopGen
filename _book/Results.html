4.2 Genome Assembly and Annotation

The assembly pipeline was successful and generated contiguous, high-quality draft assemblies for the Xaj and Xap populations. The Xaj assemblies were comprised of 61.4 ± 7.4 contigs with a length of 5.14 ± 0.07 Mb and an N50 of 167.1 Kb ± 27.7 Kb. The Xaj PGAP annotations yielded 4,485.8 ± 79.1 genes and 4,217 ± 73 proteins. These assembly and annotation results are consistent with other Xaj assemblies (n=32) that were included in Table S1 described in Assis et al. (2021). The results from CheckM run on the polished assemblies are found in Table 4.2.

The Xap assemblies were comprised of 34.9 ± 7 contigs with a length of 4.97 ± 0.02 Mb and an N50 317.6 ± 139.2 Kb. The Xap PGAP annotations yielded 4,276.7 ± 22.6 genes and 3,987 ± 24.2 proteins. These assembly and annotation reports were consistent with other publicly available Xap genomic resources (accessed August 24, 2024). The results from PGAP are found in 4.3.

All the ANI reports indicated the isolates were CONFIRMED with HIGH confidence in the assigned taxonomy with \(\geq 98.081%\) (Xaj) and \(\geq 97.299%\) (Xap) identity to Xanthomonas arboricola pv. juglandis (GCA_001013475.1, ASM101347v1) in ani_summary_table.tsv. CheckM reports indicated \(\geq 97.03%\) and 98.62% completeness and \(\leq 1.32%\) and 1.87% contamination for the Xaj and Xap populations, respectively.

Overall, I’m satisfied with the genome assembly and annotation results of the Xap and Xaj populations. Our results are consistent with other published assemblies and will serve as a reliable resource for future studies.

4.3 In Vitro Copper Sensitivity

We identified 77 Xaj strains that were resistant to copper, the remaining Xaj and all Xap strains were sensitive. Resistance was characterized by growth at \(\geq 5,000 ppm\) (4.1, 4.2, 4.3).

I identified five strains, XAJ321, XAJ341, XAJ939, XAJ1614, XAJ355, that did not align with the genomic data. These strains were all found to be resistant but did not encode copper resistance-related genes (see SECTION 4.4 Pangenome results). I removed these strains from the study and re-ran the pangenome and phylogenetic analyses. These updated runs have “XAJ_115” in the name. The results throughout this book will reflect these changes.

4.4.1.1 Pangenome Partitions

A pangenome graph was generated with Gephi using the pangenomeGraph.gexf as the input file. Nodes represent gene families (clusters of homologous genes across the input genomes). Edges connect gene families that are genomic neighbors (i.e., their sequences are adjacent in at least one genome). The graph is partitioned into categories—persistent, shell, and cloud—based on how frequently gene families appear across genomes and their neighborhood relationships. This structure resembles a “subway map” of genomic diversity, allowing you to visualize the core and variable components of a species’ pangenome. The ‘persistent,’ ‘shell,’ and ‘cloud’ genomes constitute 69.95%, 19.14%, and 10.91% of the pangenome, respectively (4.4).

Figure 4.4: Pangenome graph of the 115 CA strains. ‘Persistent’: purple; ‘shell’: orange; ‘cloud’: green.

4.4.1.2 Copper Resistance-Related Genes

Various copper resistance-related genes have been characterized in Xanthomonas and in Xaj. These genes include: copL, copA, copB, copC, copD, and copK (Lee et al. (1994); cesbron2015; Marin et al. (2019); Assis et al. (2021)). The copA and copC genes encode periplasmic copper-binding proteins, conferring partial copper resistance in P. syringae (Marin et al. (2019)). In X. citri, Richard et al. (2017) reported plasmid-bore copper resistance genes termed copABCD, however in Xaj these genes are found on the chromosome (Lee et al. (1994); Giovanardi et al. (2016); Assis et al. (2021)). In both copper-sensitive (CuS) and copper-resistant (CuR) Xanthomonas strains, the chromosomal genes cohL, cohA, and cohB, involved in copper homeostasis, exhibit homology to cop resistance genes located on plasmids (Franklin Behlau et al. (2011); F. Behlau, Fonseca, and Belasque Jr (2016)).

The PPanGGoLiN analysis successfully identified copper resistance-related genes as well as the copper homeostatsis genes. The genes associated with copper resistance are copL family metal-binding regulatory protein, copper resistance system multicopper oxidase ( copA ), copper resistance protein B ( copB ), copper homeostasis periplasmic binding protein copC, copper homeostasis membrane protein copD, and copK family periplasmic copper-binding protein. Each of these six genes are present in the ‘shell’ or ‘cloud’ partitions and while the respective copL, copA, copB homeostasis genes are present in the ‘persistent’ genome and found in all 115 strains (4.4).

A total of 77 strains were found to encode copper resistance genes ( copA, copB, copC, copD, and copK) in various combinations and copy number. These same 77 strains were also characterized as CuR based on the in vitro sensitivity assay.

4.4.1.3 Type III Effectors

The presence/absence matrix (matrix_Xaj_115.xlsx) was used to identify type III effectors (T3Es) by searching for the term “tpye III secretion system effector” in the ‘Annotations’ column. I was able to identify 18 effectors using this method. After cross-referencing the tBLASTn & BLASTp results, I was able to identify 4 more that did not have “type III secretion system effector” in the annotation but were determined to be T3Es by Method 3 (3.6).

Only two T3Es were identified in the presence/absence matrix, XopR and XopAH, that were not captured by Method 3 (LINK METHOD 3 RESULTS TABLE).

  • The XAJ283A XopAH gene (ACET0C_003701) was present in the raw BLASTp and tBLASTn outputs but exhibited ~28% identity with E-values of 5.11e-33 and 1.53e-36 to WP_011037263.1 (XopAH), respectively. I performed a web-based BLASTp with XAJ283A’s XopAH gene ACET0C_003701 it yielded >99% ID to X. arboricola “XopAH/AvrB family type III secretion system effector” (WP_372159172.1, WP_080591327.1, and WP_258077835.1) and to Xaj “type III effector protein AvrB3” (AKU50911.1). The reason that this gene was not captured in the BLAST analyses is likely because the reference XopAH WP_011037263.1 is a X. campestris protein and exhibited too much sequence variation to the query Xaj sequences.

  • The XAJ283A XopR gene (ACET0C_000329) was present in the raw BLASTp and tBLASTn outputs but exhibited ~37% and ~44% identity with E-values of 2.12e-60 and 4.89e-36 to WP_011260681.1 (XopR), respectively. I performed a web-based BLASTp with XAJ283A’s XopR gene ACET0C_000329 it yielded 100% and 99.76 % idenetity to X. arboricola “type III secretion system effector protein XopR” (WP_104538897.1) and to the X. arboricola pv. corylina “type III secretion system effector protein XopR” (WIX26666.1), respectively. 4.4.1.4 Modules, Spots, and RGPs (LEFT OFF HERE!!!)

Modules

  • Definition: Modules in PPanGGOLiN are conserved groups of genes, typically from the variable (shell and cloud) genome, that tend to co-occur and are co-localized across multiple genomes in the pangenome. These are often hypothesized to represent functional units that are gained or lost together.

  • How They’re Generated: Modules are predicted using the panModule method, which analyzes patterns of gene co-occurrence and genomic neighborhood within RGPs. The process involves identifying clusters of genes that consistently appear together in the variable regions of the pangenome.

Spots

  • Definition: Spots, or Spots of Insertion, are groups of RGPs from different genomes that share conserved flanking persistent genes, indicating a common insertion site in the pangenome. They represent hotspots of genomic variability.

  • How They’re Generated: After RGPs are identified, the spot command groups them into Spots based on shared genomic context (persistent gene borders). This clustering reflects where RGPs tend to integrate across the pangenome.

RGPs (Regions of Genomic Plasticity)

  • Definition: RGPs are genomic regions in individual genomes that consist of genes from the shell and cloud partitions (i.e., non-persistent genes). These regions are typically associated with horizontal gene transfer (HGT) and often correspond to Genomic Islands (GIs).

  • How They’re Generated: The panRGP method identifies RGPs by scanning each genome and detecting clusters of consecutive shell or cloud genes, using a score-based algorithm to define boundaries. Persistent genes flanking these regions help delineate them.

Interconnection

  • Modules, RGPs, and Spots are interconnected: Modules are often subsets of genes within RGPs, and Spots group RGPs across genomes. The –spot_modules option ties these together by associating Modules with specific Spots and RGPs, enabling a deeper understanding of how conserved gene clusters relate to variable genomic regions.

  • Purpose: Together, these features allow PPanGGOLiN to depict microbial diversity by partitioning the pangenome into persistent, shell, and cloud components, then analyzing the variable regions for patterns of conservation and plasticity.
